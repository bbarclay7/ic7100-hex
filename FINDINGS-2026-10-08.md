# Findings, 2026-10-07/08: why the panel was inaudible, and what CESSB buys

Radio: IC-7100 on 80 m, 3.758 MHz LSB. Monitor: FT-710 on the same antenna switch (switch leakage), AGC off,
RF gain lowered; audio recorded on hamtop1 with `ffmpeg.exe` (dshow, "FT710 Microphone"). Numbers are
signal-over-noise of active speech in dB (noise = median level of each clip), single takes of "radio test AK6MJ".

## Root causes found (panel transmitted little or nothing)

1. **Audio processes never recovered.** `arecord`/`aplay` died when the radio was unplugged (it was moved to
   another computer and back) and nothing respawned them, so the panel streamed browser audio into a dead pipe
   (log said "N frames sent to the radio", radio saw nothing: ALC 11, Po 0). Fixed in `ic7100ctl/audio.py`: each
   pipe respawns itself every 2 s; tests in `tests/test_audio.py` fail on the old code and pass on the new.
2. **The browser's microphone was not the one the dropdown showed.** On fallback to the default input Chrome
   reports deviceId `"default"`, so the dropdown stayed on the saved choice. Bluetooth headsets also delivered
   digital silence (-90 dBFS) until awake. Fixed: dropdown shows the real device (matched by id, then name),
   saved choice is remembered by id and name, `[tx]` log line names the mic and its peak level.
3. **The remote antenna tuner's battery was dead,** so 80 m was badly matched (SWR ~2.5-3:1) and nobody
   (including a public receiver 21 miles away) heard 10 W. After the fix SWR reads ~1.0.
4. **Handset mic gain was 99%,** driving the radio's ALC to ~150 (limit ~120).

Ruled out: the radio's DATA mode (verified on-radio while keyed), the radio's USB audio input (a 1 kHz tone from
hex gave ALC 52 / Po 41 and a clean tone on the FT-710), a stale ALSA card number, antenna switch position.

## Mic gain (handset path only) at the real operating power, 100%

| mic gain | ALC peak |
|---|---|
| 40% | 117 |
| 45% | 127 |
| 55% | 128 |

Chosen: **40%** (peaks at/just under ALC ~120). At 10% power the radio's ALC acts even at 30% gain, so gain must be
set at the operating power; the ALC reading alone is a poor guide at low power.

## Head to head at 100% power

| run | over noise | peak (dBFS) | peak-to-average |
|---|---|---|---|
| A handset, mic gain 40% | 40.6 dB | -26.9 | 16.4 dB |
| B panel, **CESSB on** (+12 dB) | **43.9 dB** | -27.0 | **13.0 dB** |
| C panel, CESSB off | 42.4 dB | -27.7 | 13.7 dB |
| D handset, radio speech compressor 50%, mic gain 40% | 42.6 dB | -26.3 | 15.1 dB |

- Panel with CESSB is **+3.3 dB** over the handset (about half an S-unit, double average power); the plain panel
  is +1.8 dB. At equal peak, CESSB over the plain panel is +0.7 dB (+1.5 dB by raw level). Single takes: treat
  these as direction and rough size, +/- 1 dB.
- The radio's own compressor adds about +2.0 dB to the handset, but its ALC read 140 (over the ~120 limit), so
  the mic gain would have to come down; the panel with CESSB stays about 1.3 dB ahead of it with ALC ~101.
  CESSB's lower distortion is inferred from that ALC difference, not measured.
- Panel controls for the radio's compressor (on/off, level; hand mic only) were added: CI-V `16 44` and `14 0E`.
- CESSB booster +18 dB gave no more than +12 dB (tested at 10% power); simulation agrees (diminishing returns).
  Benefit depends on mic level (none for very quiet mics). The slider is capped at +18.
- Panel at 100 W: Po 227-ish (full power), ALC 97-101, SWR ~1.0, supply min ~13 V. No change to the radio's USB MOD
  level is needed.

## CESSB: what is and is not shown

Reference: https://shop.rf.guru/pages/what-is-cessb ("Done well, it lets the useful speech waveform sit closer to a declared
peak-envelope-power ceiling. Done badly, or followed by a nonlinear amplifier, it can still produce distortion and unwanted
emissions ... The engineering evidence is a controlled before-and-after test at the final RF output.") It asks for a two-tone IMD
test and an occupied-bandwidth comparison with identical audio and the same final PEP, measured after the last device that can
distort, and warns not to judge by the ALC bar or average power alone.

What we have is average level and crest factor from the FT-710's demodulated audio (+3.3 dB average at equal peak vs the
handset; ALC about 101 with CESSB vs 117-152 for the others) plus an audio-domain simulation. Those are the kind of evidence the
page says is not enough. Not measured: IMD, occupied bandwidth and splatter at the RF output (the FT-710's IF filter hides
anything wider than about 3 kHz), the IC-7100's real transmit passband (assumed about 300-2800 Hz), or on-air reports. Treat
"CESSB is better" as plausible and unproven until that controlled test is done.

## CESSB RF test, stage 1 (FT-710 recording of a known waveform; IC-7100 into a 50 W dummy load, LSB 3.758 MHz, 25% power)

Method: synthetic test waveforms (files in `sdr-tests/cessb-rf/`, with a chirp and a 1 kHz pilot up front) are played from hex into
the radio's codec while keyed; the FT-710 records the demodulated result; `analyze_rf.py` / `imd.py` compare it with what was sent.
Tones: 900 + 1400 Hz, IMD3 products at 400 and 1900 Hz. The FT-710 hears the transmitter through the coax switch's leakage.

- The radio's transmit passband is about **281-2719 Hz** (3 dB points). The CESSB filter's 2800 Hz upper edge is slightly wide.
- The two radios' carriers differed by +1.4 Hz; the two sound devices' clocks by about -190 ppm (both removed in the analysis).
- The radio **preserved the crest factor** of a speech-like waveform (15.8 dB recorded vs 15.1 dB ideal after its passband).
- **The radio's ALC regulates its output over a wide range of drive.** Two-tone sweep, peak level into the codec vs result:
  -1.4 to -13.4 dBFS: output level flat within 1.5 dB, IMD3 about -30 dBc (-29.5 to -31.2). Knee near -19 dBFS. Below -25 dBFS the
  output tracks the input 1:1 and IMD3 is about -45 dBc (limited by the measurement noise floor). So the panel's audio (peaks
  about -2 dBFS) has been driving this radio 15-20 dB past the point where it is linear, with the ALC doing the regulating.
  At 100% power the knee is higher (about 6 dB, extrapolated, not measured: the dummy load cannot take 100 W).
- This is the situation the CESSB reference warns about: a downstream ALC that "can alter the waveform that the DSP produced".
  The CESSB benefit so far (+3.3 dB average) was measured with the radio in that regime.
- Next: repeat CESSB vs plain speech at drive levels in the radio's linear region, and decide on a panel TX-trim control.

## CESSB RF test, stage 2 (IC-7100 at 40% into the dummy load, same method)

- **Where the radio reaches its set power.** Two-tone, output level vs input peak: at 25% the output is flat from -1.4 to -13 dBFS
  (knee near -16); at 40% it tracks the input from about -11 dBFS down. IMD3 at 40%: -30 dBc at -10 dBFS, -34 at -19, -38 at -22,
  -44 at -25. So distortion falls steadily as the drive is backed off from the point where the radio is at full set power; the
  radio's own IMD3 at full PEP is about -30 dBc whatever the input. The knee moves up with the power setting, so at 100% the panel's
  roughly -2 dBFS peaks are probably much closer to right than at 25% (extrapolated, not measured: the load is 50 W).
- **Equal digital peak is not equal RF peak.** The radio's ~300-2700 Hz passband shaves the sharp peaks of unprocessed speech (a
  peak of -11.4 dBFS into the codec came back 7.7 dB lower than the same peak of CESSB speech). Compare at equal *received* peak.
- **Average level at equal received peak, vs unprocessed speech:** CESSB +12 dB: +2.1 dB (linear drive: +1.5 dB); CESSB +18 dB: +4.5 dB
  (+3.5 dB). Received crest factor: plain 16.2 dB, CESSB12 14.2 dB, CESSB18 11.8 dB (as sent: 12.9 and 9.4): the radio's filter gives some
  of the crest-factor reduction back, so the processor should be designed around the radio's real passband (about 281-2719 Hz).
- **Not established: whether CESSB adds distortion in the radio.** My waveform-match metric (per-block normalised correlation) came out
  at 0.7-0.8 in every case regardless of drive, so it measures the radio's phase response, not distortion, and was discarded.
  A proper distortion test needs the linear transfer function removed (or a notched-noise / multi-tone test) and a wide RF capture
  for splatter beyond the FT-710's filter. Still open.

## CESSB RF test, stage 3: adjacent-band (splatter) check (IC-7100 40% into the dummy load, FT-710 retuned)

Method: no-preamble speech files (`np_*`, from `NOPRE=1 node gen.js`), each played three times at -25.4 / -11.4 / -1.4 dBFS peak. The FT-710 recorded
each once on 3.758 MHz LSB (A, the wanted signal) and once on 3.755 MHz LSB (B, which sees roughly 3.3-6 kHz below the carrier). Metric: total
power above the recording's own noise (first 2 s), divided by the 8 s of speech; B minus A is the adjacent level in dBc.

- B stays at about -92 to -95 dB (full-scale reference) at every drive level and for every version, while A rises from -71 to -45 dB. B does not
  scale with the transmitted signal, so it is the FT-710's own leakage/noise floor, not splatter.
- At the hottest drive (peaks -1.4 dBFS): B - A = -41.7 dB plain, -44.5 dB CESSB +12, -47.7 dB CESSB +18. These are floor-limited bounds, and the
  floor is the same for all three, so the differences come from A getting stronger.
- Result: **no adjacent-band energy was detected above the measurement floor for any version; the test can only exclude splatter above about -42 dBc**
  (a real transmitter is usually specified near -30 dBc at 3 kHz offset, so this is a weak pass, not proof of cleanliness). Some low-drive cells
  show negative excess (noise subtraction), which is why they print as -300 dB. A cleaner statement needs a wide RF capture with a proper receiver.
- **Visual observation (operator, FT-710 waterfall during the whole series):** span about ±10 kHz, scope level set so the noise floor was just
  visible; nothing appeared outside the intended signal's passband at any point, for any version or drive level. Qualitative (no recorded
  screenshots, scope scaling not logged), but it covers 10 kHz either side and the scope floor is probably below my recording floor, so it
  supports the measurement above.

## Monitor receiver setting (confirmed by operator, 2026-10-09)
FT-710 front end was on **AMP1** (preamp on) for the entire CESSB RF series (stages 1-3), with AGC off and RF gain lowered. Not recorded at the time;

## CESSB RF test, stage 4: 100 W into the antenna (2026-10-09, daytime, 80 m local)

Setup change: antenna on (SWR 1:1 by the radio's meter and by CI-V, 10/50/100% tone steps), power 100%, FT-710 front end on **IPO** (was AMP1 for
stages 1-3), AGC off, RF gain knob at about 2 o'clock (not moved during the series; a lower setting was not tested). Same files (`np_*`), same
recording method; recordings are `h100_*` (two-tone), `h100A_*` (FT-710 on 3.758) and `h100B_*` (FT-710 on 3.755, scope 3750-3760).

- **Two-tone sweep at 100%:** the output stops rising at about -10 dBFS peak into the codec (13.4 -> 10.4 dBFS: +1.9 dB; above that +0.1 dB). IMD3
  is -28 to -30 dBc once saturated (-27 at -4.4 dBFS), the same as at 40%. **The knee does not move up with power** (earlier extrapolation was wrong): it
  is at about -11 to -13 dBFS at both 40% and 100%. The panel's speech peaks (about -2 dBFS) run roughly 10 dB into the ALC at either power.
  Below -30 dBFS drive the results are floor-limited (about -40 dBc), because the FT-710 audio is only about -30 dBFS peak even at saturation.
- **Speech at the hot drive (peak -1.4 dBFS), received average vs plain:** CESSB +12 +2.7 dB (+2.1 at equal received peak), CESSB +18 +4.2 dB (+3.8 at equal
  peak). Crest 15.0 / 13.0 / 11.2 dB. Agrees with the 40% result (+2.1 / +4.5) within about 0.7 dB; single takes, treat as +/-1 dB.
- **Adjacent band (3.755) vs in-band (3.758), hot drive:** -43.7 dB plain, -43.1 CESSB +12, -46.6 CESSB +18. The adjacent level rises about 5-9 dB between
  the quietest and hottest drives (it was flat at 40%), so a little real spill is plausible. No version is clearly worse (differences of 1-3 dB are
  within single-take error); CESSB +18 ratio is best only because its in-band level is highest.
- **Operator's waterfall (FT-710, 3750-3760 kHz):** a slight tail below 3755 tapering to the noise floor at about 3754 (4 kHz below the carrier,
  about 1.3 kHz beyond the radio's 2.72 kHz passband edge), consistent with the radio's own IMD3 spill at -28 to -30 dBc. Whether the tail differed between versions was not noted.
- Caveats: single takes, FT-710 leakage floor about -42 to -47 dBc, no calibrated spectrum, speech is synthetic.
- **Receiver-overload check (RF gain turned down about a third, 100%, FT-710 on 3.758; recordings `lowg_*`):** the received two-tone level fell 17.4 dB
  (55.8 -> 38.4 dB) and the noise floor 3.9 dB, yet IMD3 was unchanged: -27.2 / -28.3 dBc against -27.0 / -28.1 dBc before (IM5 -34.4 vs -35.0).
  A receiver front end generating the products would have lost about 2 dB of IMD per 1 dB of input (about 35 dB here). **The distortion is the
  transmitter's, not the monitor's**, at least at the 2 o'clock setting and above the recording floor of about -35 dBc. Hot CESSB +18 speech: Po 208, ALC 92.
  The adjacent-band pass (3.755) was not repeated at the lower gain.
